Module 1 — CI Basics

Learning outcomes

  • Explain what Contact Investigation is and why it matters.
  • Apply confidentiality rules in real-world situations.
  • Describe the CI workflow from referral to closure.
  • Identify priorities and safety concerns during investigations.
1) What CI is

Contact Investigation (CI) is a structured public health process used to stop infections from spreading.

  • Interview the person diagnosed (index case).
  • Identify contacts who may have been exposed.
  • Notify contacts without revealing the index patient’s identity or diagnosis.
  • Support contacts to test and treat (through clinic/doctor processes).
  • Document actions and outcomes (IR + FR) and report indicators.
2) Confidentiality & ethics

Non-negotiable rule

Never reveal the index client’s diagnosis or identity to a partner/contact.

  • Use privacy in conversations (no public spaces, avoid loud speaking).
  • Secure paper records; don’t leave forms in vehicles or open desks.
  • Use codes/standard terms required by the programme.
  • Only share information on a need-to-know basis within the health team.
3) Basic CI workflow
  1. Receive referral (from clinic, lab, doctor, programme notification).
  2. Prepare (review available info, plan safe approach, materials).
  3. Interview (build rapport, explain purpose, collect contacts & locating info).
  4. Assign contacts and open FRs; document attempts/outcomes.
  5. Link contacts to services (testing, treatment, counseling).
  6. Close case with complete documentation and reporting.
4) Safety & professional practice
  • Assess risk of violence or conflict before partner notification.
  • Use safe meeting places; inform supervisor of field plans when needed.
  • Stay respectful, non-judgmental, and calm.
  • Document objectively (facts, dates, outcomes).

Suggested time

2 hours

Read, expand each section, take notes, and discuss sample scenarios.

Quick memory anchors

  • “Protect identity”
  • “Document everything”
  • “Notify safely”
  • “Link to testing/treatment”

Module 2 — Forms & Records

Learning outcomes

  • State the purpose of the IR and FR.
  • Complete core fields accurately and clearly.
  • Recognize common documentation errors and how to avoid them.
  • Demonstrate safe, confidential record-handling practices.
1) Interview Record (IR) — what it captures
  • Index client identification details (as per programme rules).
  • Key interview details (date, clinic/location, interviewer).
  • Risk/exposure window and contacts named.
  • Locating information and referral information.
  • Key notes that support follow-up (objective, not judgmental).
2) Field Record (FR) — what it tracks
  • Contact locator details (address, phone, directions).
  • Every attempt to reach the contact (date/time/method).
  • Outcome status (located, tested, treated, refused, not found, moved).
  • Linkage actions (clinic referral, counseling, appointment support).
3) Common documentation mistakes (and fixes)
  • Missing dates → Always date every action and attempt.
  • Vague addresses → Add landmarks/directions, not just a community name.
  • Subjective language → Write facts (“client stated…”, “contact not at address”).
  • Confidentiality slips → Never write notes that reveal diagnosis to non-team members.
  • No outcome → Every FR should end with a clear final status if closed.

Module 3 — STI Management (CI perspective)

Learning outcomes

  • Explain syndromic management at a high level (CI support role).
  • Apply STI coding principles to protect confidentiality.
  • Describe linkage steps (testing, treatment, follow-up, reporting).
  • Recognize key syndromes where partner notification is essential.
1) What “syndromic management” means

Syndromic management treats based on symptoms and signs (syndromes) when lab confirmation is not immediately available. The CI role supports partner notification, referral, and follow-up documentation.

2) STI coding (for confidentiality)
  • Codes support privacy and consistent reporting.
  • Use standard programme codes and avoid “labeling language” in notes.
  • Only authorized clinical staff confirm diagnosis; CI supports investigations.
3) Key partner-notification syndromes
  • Genital ulcer syndrome (includes syphilis concerns)
  • Urethral discharge
  • Vaginal discharge with risk factors
  • Other conditions flagged as priority by programme guidance
4) Linking contacts to services
  1. Notify contact without revealing index identity/diagnosis.
  2. Encourage clinic visit for testing and treatment advice.
  3. Support appointment making / directions / service navigation.
  4. Document outcome: tested, treated, refused, not found, etc.
  5. Close FR when outcome is finalized or investigation is ended per protocol.

Suggested time

1.5 hours

Focus on CI support actions: referral, follow-up, documentation, reporting.
