Contact Investigation Basics

What is Contact Investigation?

Contact Investigation (CI) is a public health strategy used to break the chain of transmission of sexually transmitted infections (STIs) and other communicable diseases. It is an acknowledged method for combating the spread of sexually transmitted infections.

Core Strategy

The strategy is to track sexual partners, while maintaining their confidentiality, and test and treat those who are infected. Each newly identified infected contact becomes the starting point (index case) of a new contact tracing process.

Breaking the Chain of Transmission

Transmission or reinfection is minimized through referral of sex partners for diagnosis and treatment. This activity may be undertaken by:

  1. The Client: Patient assumes responsibility and plays an active role in bringing contacts for evaluation and treatment.
  2. Health Provider (Assisted Partner Notification Services): With consent, providers support disclosure or anonymously inform partners of potential exposure, then offer testing.

Core Responsibilities

Contact Tracing: Identifying and notifying sexual partners
Partner Notification: Disclosing potential exposure to partners
Linkage to Treatment: Ensuring contacts access STI treatment
Risk-Reduction Counseling: Providing education on safer practices
Record Completion: Accurate documentation of all activities

Program Goal

This guide promotes standardization in contact investigation across Jamaica, enhancing outcomes and contributing to the reduction of STIs.

Confidentiality in CI Work

Why Confidentiality is Paramount

Confidentiality fosters trust and ensures individuals feel safe sharing sensitive information. By protecting privacy, CIs can effectively identify and notify contacts while maintaining the integrity of the process.

Risks of Breaches

The lack of confidentiality may contribute to social harm including:

  • Stigma and discrimination
  • Intimate partner violence
  • Loss of trust in healthcare system
  • Program failure and reduced participation

Strategies to Protect Confidentiality

  1. Interview the patient or contact in private settings
  2. Code data to protect the patient's diagnosis (use STI codes)
  3. Store patient data securely per MOHW protocols for health records
  4. Ensure access to patient records follows established protocols
  5. Use professional judgment and sensitivity in all interactions

Legal Framework

The Secrecy Act and the Data Protection Act govern all healthcare workers, including Contact Investigators, and provide the framework for handling patient information.

Professional Responsibility

The character, judgment, integrity, and sensitivity—essentially the professionalism—of the Contact Investigator is the best guarantee of privacy and confidentiality.

Confidentiality Breaches

Protocol for Breaches

Alleged breaches in confidentiality are a real threat to the STI programme success. Such instances should be:

  • Investigated promptly
  • Appropriately managed
  • Used to minimize harm to persons and the programme
  • Addressed through retraining if needed

CI Roles & Responsibilities

Primary Duties of a Contact Investigator

Key Activities

  • Locate and interview individuals diagnosed with STIs, particularly syphilis and HIV/AIDS
  • Support surveillance and screening protocols for STIs in clinical settings
  • Identify and engage individuals eligible for Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP)
  • Conduct contact tracing for all clients diagnosed with HIV, STIs, or TB
  • Ensure early diagnosis, timely review, and linkage to care for all persons living with HIV (PLHIV) within four weeks of diagnosis
  • Conduct annual follow-up with all PLHIV, eliciting new contacts for tracing as needed
  • Re-interview all unsuppressed PLHIV clients every six months for updated contact tracing
  • Interview PLHIV with drug-resistant HIV every six months to update contact lists

Performance Standards & Timelines

Critical Timelines

  • Priority STI cases: Assign to CI within 72 hours
  • Patients returning to care: Interview within 5 working days of return
  • Patients lost to follow-up: Locate within 2 weeks of referral
  • Monthly reports: Due 5th working day of following month
  • Class 1 notifications: Within 24 hours of suspicion/confirmation

Investigation Priority Levels

Priority A Conditions

These must receive immediate investigation:

  • HIV/AIDS (C13)
  • Pregnant women with reactive serology
  • Early congenital syphilis (< 1 yr.) (A7)
  • Infectious Syphilis (A1, A2, A3)
  • Ophthalmia Neonatorum
  • STI in Childhood

  • Transgender people
  • Sex workers
  • People who use drugs
  • People in prisons and closed settings

  • Secondary Syphilis: 6 months maximum IP

STI Interview Record (IR)

Purpose of the Interview Record

The Interview Record provides the information used for summary statistics and serves to:

  1. Compile information about the patient's contacts and suspected contacts for analytical and planning purposes
  2. Inform the parish surveillance team about characteristics of patients diagnosed with priority infections
  3. Help CIs prepare the Monthly Summary Statistical Report (MSSR)
  4. Provide a permanent record of interviews performed and their outcome for future investigations as necessary

IR Routing

Three Copies

The Interview Record is completed in triplicate:

  1. Index Case (IC): Submitted to parish CI supervisor following interview
  2. Case Closure (CC): Submitted when case is closed (all FRs dispositioned)
  3. Case File (CF): Permanent record retained by CI

Critical Period Contacts

Important Rule

Unless otherwise approved by supervisor, ALL critical period contacts will be initiated into Field Records.

Key Field Explanations

Contact Period Start Date: Date when patient's interview process commenced
Critical Period: Maximum duration during which patient may have been infected and infectious
Cluster Suspects: Named non-sex partners outside critical period

STI Field Record (FR)

Purpose of the Field Record

The STI Field Record Form is for the investigation of contacts. It serves to:

  1. Guide the investigation in the field
  2. Inform the CI and supervisor about work to be done (open work) and work completed (closed work)
  3. Provide a permanent record for the CI for future reference

FR Routing Mechanism

Three Copies

  1. First Copy: Attach to IR and submit through parish supervisors
  2. Second Copy: Dispositioned copies submitted weekly to supervisor
  3. Third Copy: Used in field, filed securely for follow-up

Case Closure Criteria

When to Close a Case

Cases are generally closed when:

  • All Field Records (FRs) have been dispositioned
  • At least 4 reasonable attempts made
  • Minimum of 3 calls and 3 visits within 3 months
  • Supervisor concurs with closure

Important Definitions

Possible Case: Person with reported reactive serology (lab, ANC, blood bank)
Cluster Suspect: Named non-sex partner with lesions suggestive of syphilis
Exposure Frequency: Frequency of sexual contact (e.g., 1x/week, 5x/month)

Form Routing & Submission

Complete Form Flow Process

Step-by-Step Flow

  1. Interview Record Completion: IR completed in triplicate after interviewing index case
  2. IR Submission: IC copy submitted to Parish CI supervisor for review
  3. Field Record Initiation: FR created for each contact with locating information
  4. FR Submission: First FR copy attached to IR; second copy submitted weekly when dispositioned
  5. Case Closure: When all contacts dispositioned, CC copy of IR submitted with all FRs attached
  6. Monthly Reporting: MSSR submitted by 5th working day of following month

Digital Transformation

CI Form Digitization

With digitization of interview records:

  • Combined forms for IC, CC, CF completed digitally
  • Parish CI supervisor can pull reports for review and monitoring
  • Real-time access to data and reports
  • Reduced paperwork and improved data accuracy

Monthly Reporting Requirements

Critical Deadlines

  • CI Monthly Report: Due 5th working day of following month
  • STI Monthly Report: Submitted through DHIS2 database
  • Class 1 Notifications: Within 24 hours of suspicion/confirmation
  • Priority Cases: Assign to CI within 72 hours of identification

Database Integration

Monthly CI and STI reports are entered into the District Health Information 2 (DHIS2) database. Data should be reviewed by Senior CI and Medical Officer of Health before submission to national level at HIV/STI/TB Unit.

Reporting Hierarchy

Reporting Structure

  • Contact Investigator: Completes IR/FR, submits to Parish CI
  • Parish CI Supervisor: Reviews forms, submits to Regional CI
  • Regional CI (RCI): Submits monthly reports to regional focal point
  • Central Level: Programme Development Officer, MOHW

Special Reports

Congenital Syphilis Report: For infants born to syphilis-positive mothers
Ophthalmia Neonatorum Report: Class 1 notifiable disease
Paediatric HIV Report: Priority A infection requiring immediate investigation
HIV Confidential Reporting Form: For all HIV-positive diagnoses

Form Completion Tips & Best Practices

Common Errors to Avoid

Critical Mistakes

  • Missing Dates: Incomplete dates prevent calculation of investigation timelines
  • Incorrect Parish Codes: Always use 3-letter parish codes (e.g., STT, KSA)
  • Blank Required Fields: Forms returned for completion waste time
  • Confusing Contacts Named vs FRs Initiated: These are different numbers
  • Incomplete STI Codes: All patient infections must be recorded

Best Practices for Accuracy

Accuracy Tips

  1. Verify Information: Double-check names, addresses, and dates
  2. Use Official Codes: Reference STI coding list when unsure
  3. Complete All Fields: If information is unknown, write "UNKNOWN"
  4. Circle Estimated Ages: Clearly indicate when age is estimated
  5. Include Aliases: Record all names patient is known by
  6. Document Attempts: Record all attempts to locate contacts

Syphilis Investigation & Management

Syphilis Stages & Clinical Features

Infectious Syphilis (Duration up to 2 years)

Primary Syphilis (A1)
  • Chancre: hard, painless sore
  • Appears 9-90 days after exposure
  • Localized lymphadenopathy
  • STS may be negative early
Secondary Syphilis (A2)
  • Symmetrical rashes (palms/soles)
  • Condylomata lata
  • Generalized lymphadenopathy
  • Systemic symptoms possible
Early Latent (A3)
  • No clinical signs/symptoms
  • Duration ≤ 2 years
  • Reactive STS
  • TRUST/VDRL ≥ 1:8

Treatment Guidelines

Benzathine Penicillin G (BPG) Treatment

  • Primary, Secondary, Early Latent: BPG 2.4 MU IM single dose
  • Late Latent, Tertiary: BPG 2.4 MU IM weekly x 3 weeks
  • Neurosyphilis: Requires longer treatment; refer to specialist
  • Penicillin Allergy: Refer to physician for alternative treatment

Follow-up Requirements

Post-Treatment Monitoring

  • Primary/Secondary: Exam and STS 3-monthly x 4
  • Early Latent: Exam and STS 6-monthly x 4, then annually
  • Late Latent/Tertiary: Annual follow-up as needed
  • Pregnant women: Monthly follow-up until delivery

Laboratory Testing

Syphilis Testing Algorithm

  1. Screening: SD Bioline (treponemal test)
  2. Confirmation: TRUST/VDRL (non-treponemal)
  3. Additional: TPPA or FTA-ABS if needed
  4. Darkfield microscopy: For moist lesions if available

Epidemiologic Treatment

Epi-Treatment Criteria

Give to contacts who:

  • Have negative syphilis serology
  • No clinical signs/symptoms
  • Within critical period of exposure
  • Exposed to infectious syphilis case

STI Coding Reference

A1: Primary Syphilis
A2: Secondary Syphilis
A3: Early Latent Syphilis
A6: All other late/late latent stages
A7: Congenital syphilis (< 1 year)

HIV Investigation & Management

HIV Investigation Guidelines

Priority A Condition

HIV/AIDS (C13) is a Priority A condition requiring immediate investigation and reporting.

Key Investigation Steps

  1. Confirm Diagnosis: Ensure positive HIV test following national algorithm
  2. Complete Class 1 Notification: Submit within 24 hours of diagnosis
  3. Interview Index Case: Complete IR and elicit contacts from infectious period
  4. Contact Tracing: Notify and test all sexual and needle-sharing contacts
  5. Linkage to Care: Ensure patient referred to treatment site same day
  6. Partner Notification: Discuss options (patient, provider, or contract referral)

HIV Testing Algorithm

National HIV Testing Strategy

  • Initial Test: HIV Determine or similar rapid test
  • Confirmatory Test: Second different rapid test (Uni-Gold, etc.)
  • Discordant Results: ELISA or Western Blot for confirmation
  • HIV Self-Testing (HIVST): OraQuick test kit available through outreach
  • Positive Self-Test: Refer to clinic for confirmatory testing and counseling

Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP)

PrEP Eligibility Criteria

Persons eligible for PrEP must:

  1. Be HIV Negative
  2. Be at substantial risk for HIV acquisition (key populations or demonstrated risk)
  3. Not show signs/symptoms of acute HIV infection
  4. Have Creatinine Clearance >60 ml/min (for oral daily PrEP)

Common STI Syndromes & Management

Urethral Discharge

Common causes: Gonorrhea, Chlamydia

Management: Ceftriaxone 500mg IM stat + Doxycycline 100mg BD x 7 days

Vaginal Discharge

Common causes: Trichomoniasis, BV, Candidiasis

Management: Based on clinical findings and lab tests

Genital Ulcer Disease (GUD)

Common causes: Herpes, Syphilis, Chancroid

Management: Treat empirically + test for syphilis and HIV

Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID)

Diagnosis: Lower abdominal pain + cervical motion tenderness + adnexal tenderness

Management: Ceftriaxone 500mg IM stat + Doxycycline 100mg BD x 14 days + Metronidazole 500mg BD x 14 days

Pediatric STI Investigations

Congenital Syphilis (CS) Investigation

Priority Investigation

Congenital syphilis should be investigated immediately using the Congenital Syphilis Report Form.

Investigation Procedure:

  1. Test and treat the mother
  2. Test and treat all maternal contacts
  3. Refer infant to physician for evaluation and monthly follow-up
  4. Complete Congenital Syphilis Investigation Report Form immediately
  5. Submit form to site supervisor weekly

Ophthalmia Neonatorum (ON)

Definition & Investigation

Definition: Mucoid, mucopurulent, or purulent discharge from newborn's eyes (≤ 1 month)

Investigation Steps:

  1. Test and treat mother
  2. Test and treat contacts if history of disease
  3. Complete Class 1 notification within 24 hours
  4. Submit Ophthalmia Neonatorum Report Form weekly

Paediatric HIV Investigation

Priority A Infection

Paediatric HIV requires immediate investigation using Paediatric HIV Investigation Form.

Investigation Steps:

  1. Counsel and test the mother
  2. Counsel and test all maternal contacts
  3. Refer mother and infant to physician for monthly follow-up
  4. Educate about PMTCT (Prevention of Mother-to-Child Transmission)
  5. Complete Paediatric HIV Report Form

Case Simulation

Case Scenario: Secondary Syphilis with Pregnancy

Patient: 22-year-old female, G2P1, 24 weeks pregnant

Presentation: Presents to antenatal clinic for routine visit. On examination, she has a generalized maculopapular rash involving palms and soles. She reports the rash appeared about 3 weeks ago and is not itchy. She denies any genital sores or discomfort.

Sexual History: Married for 3 years, monogamous relationship. Last sexual contact was with husband 1 week ago. No other partners in past 2 years. Inconsistent condom use.

Obstetric History: Previous child born at term, healthy. Current pregnancy uncomplicated until now.

Age
22 years
Gender
Female
Pregnancy Status
G2P1, 24 weeks
Presenting Complaint
Non-itchy rash on palms/soles x 3 weeks

Initial Assessment

On examination: Generalized maculopapular rash involving trunk, extremities, palms and soles. No genital lesions noted. Generalized nontender lymphadenopathy present. Vital signs normal.

Preliminary Diagnosis: Suspected secondary syphilis in pregnancy

Your Investigation Plan

Based on the case scenario, what are your immediate and follow-up actions as a Contact Investigator? (Select all that apply)

Immediate Laboratory Testing

Order syphilis serology (SD Bioline and TRUST/VDRL), HIV test, and other STI screening. This is a pregnant woman with reactive serology - Priority A condition.

Complete Interview Record (IR)

Complete IR for index case. Elicit sexual contacts from critical period (last 6 months for secondary syphilis). Include husband as contact.

Routine Follow-up Only

Schedule for next antenatal visit in 4 weeks and manage as routine case since she is asymptomatic except for rash.

Partner Notification & Testing

Discuss partner notification with patient. Husband needs testing and epidemiologic treatment if exposed within critical period.

Initiate Field Record (FR)

Create FR for husband as critical period contact. Ensure testing and treatment if indicated.

Congenital Syphilis Prevention

Ensure appropriate treatment to prevent congenital syphilis. BPG treatment must be given at least 30 days before delivery.

Delay Investigation

Wait for laboratory confirmation before taking any action to avoid unnecessary anxiety for the patient and her husband.

Simulation Results

Learning Points

  • Pregnant women with reactive serology are Priority A - require immediate investigation
  • Critical period for secondary syphilis is 6 months - all contacts within this period need evaluation
  • Congenital syphilis prevention is critical - treatment must be given ≥30 days before delivery
  • Partner notification is essential - husband needs testing and possible epidemiologic treatment
  • Documentation is key - Complete IR and FRs for proper tracking and follow-up

Key Definitions

Index Case (IC): The initially identified case of an infectious disease
Critical Period Contact: A contact exposed during the maximum infectious period
Cluster Suspect: A named non-sex partner outside the critical period who may benefit from examination
Epidemiologic Treatment: Treatment given to contacts during incubation period to prevent disease progression
Possible Case: A person with reported reactive serology from lab, ANC, or blood bank

Performance Standards

CI Performance Targets

  • 95% of new cases interviewed within 6 weeks
  • 90% of locatable contacts located and tested within 6 weeks
  • 100% of Class 1 notifications within 24 hours
  • 100% of priority cases assigned within 72 hours
  • 100% of monthly reports by 5th working day
