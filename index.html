Module 1 — CI Basics (Expanded)

Manual reference

Use the CI Field Guide for deeper reading while you learn here.

CI Field Guide references: p.8–10 (confidentiality + intro).
1) What CI is trying to achieve
  • Interrupt transmission: exposed people get testing/treatment fast.
  • Reduce reinfection: partners get treated so the index doesn’t get it back.
  • Protect privacy: strong confidentiality = better disclosure and cooperation.
  • Show evidence: records prove what was done and what happened (outcomes).
2) Confidentiality: rules + real-life wording

CI Field Guide p.8

Neutral wording examples

  • “I’m calling because you may have been exposed to an infection. We recommend you get checked.”
  • “I can’t share personal information about anyone else, but I can help you access services.”
  • “We keep this private. Your details won’t be discussed publicly.”

Avoid

  • “Your girlfriend/boyfriend gave you…”
  • “X said you…”
  • “Everybody knows you…”
3) Interview quality: what separates average vs strong CI
Average interviewStrong interview
Collects few contactsUses timeline prompts + anchors to improve recall
Weak locator infoGets landmarks, best time, work/school, alternate numbers
Vague notesObjective notes + clear next steps + outcomes plan
Client feels judgedSupportive tone → better cooperation
4) Prioritisation: who you act on first

Rule of thumb: prioritise urgent risk (pregnancy/infant), high transmission potential, and cases likely to be lost to follow-up.

  • Pregnancy + syphilis risk → urgent (prevents congenital syphilis).
  • Newborn concerns (eye discharge, suspected congenital infection) → urgent referral + documentation.
  • Hard-to-locate contacts → act early before numbers change.
5) Common mistakes (and quick fixes)
MistakeFix
Talking too much, not enough listeningUse short questions, pause, reflect back
Asking “any partners?” onceUse prompts: places, weekends, events, “on/off partner”
Not confirming privacyStart every call with a privacy check
Weak locator detailsAsk: best time, landmarks, backup number, workplace/school

Suggested time

2.5 hours

Golden rule

If your words could expose identity/diagnosis, rephrase before you speak or write.

  • Every attempt gets date/time/method/result.
  • Record “best time to find” and alternate contacts.

4) Documentation language (do vs don’t)
Do writeDo NOT write
“Contact refused services after counselling; advised clinic attendance.”“Contact ignorant and rude.”
“Client unsure of date; used anchor event for estimate.”“Client lying about dates.”

5) Quick QA checklist before you close a case
  • Is the timeline clear (dates or anchored estimates)?
  • Do contacts have usable locator details?
  • Are attempts recorded with date/time/method?
  • Are outcomes verified (tested/treated/refused/unknown)?
  • Is there a final disposition and closure note?

Biggest performance killer

Weak locator details = low outcomes, even when interviews are “done.”

Module 3 — Critical Period Interview (Teaching + Tool)

Manual reference

CI Field Guide: p.41
1) How to explain critical period (client-friendly)

“We will focus on the time when you could have passed this infection to someone. That helps protect you and protect them.”

If dates are unclear, use anchors and write your assumptions.

2) Anchors that work in Jamaica
  • “Before/after Christmas”
  • “Around school reopening”
  • “Around Carnival/Grand Market”
  • “Around birthday/anniversary”
  • “When you changed jobs / moved house”

Critical Period Calculator

Estimate using maximum infectious period plus symptom duration before treatment (see Field Guide p.41).

If unknown, enter 0 and write “unknown duration” in notes.

Suggested time

1.5 hours

Common trap

If you don’t document your assumption, the next CI can’t follow your logic.

Module 4 — STI Management (Expanded)

Manual reference

STI Manual references: p.131–133 (treatment checklist), plus syndrome sections.
1) CI role around treatment (what you do / don’t do)

Scope clarity: Clinicians prescribe. CI supports partner services, linkage, prevention education, and documentation.

  • Confirm partner notification plan and assist tracing.
  • Support contacts to attend clinic for testing/treatment.
  • Prevention messages to reduce reinfection.
  • Document outcomes clearly (verified where possible).
2) STIs covered in services (broad list)
  • Syphilis (all stages) + congenital
  • Gonorrhoea, chlamydia
  • Trichomoniasis / BV, candidiasis
  • Genital herpes (HSV), genital warts (HPV)
  • Chancroid, LGV, granuloma inguinale (where relevant)
  • PID, epididymo-orchitis
  • ON (newborn eye infection)
3) Treatment awareness (CI-level summary — verify in STI Manual)

STI Manual p.131–133

Important: This is CI-level awareness to understand workflow. Exact regimens/alternatives/pregnancy adjustments must be verified in the STI Manual.

ConditionWhat CI should knowCI follow-up priority
GonorrhoeaOften treated same-day; partners need evaluation and treatmentFast partner tracing; verify clinic attendance
ChlamydiaCourse treatment; adherence mattersPrevention of reinfection; follow-up messaging
SyphilisStage determines management and timeframe for contactsCorrect critical period; partner services priority
PIDCan be urgent and needs close follow-upLink quickly; ensure partner services are addressed
HSV / HPVOften counselling + symptom control focusPrevention, stigma-sensitive support, follow-up when needed
4) Reinfection prevention: the key message set
  • “If your partner doesn’t get treated, you can get it back.”
  • “Follow the clinic advice about when to restart sex.”
  • “Use condoms every time to lower risk.”
  • “Come back if symptoms return.”
5) Mini-scenario: when a contact refuses

Scenario: You notify a contact. They say: “I don’t want no clinic.”

Best next moveWhy
Offer a calm, private explanation + clinic options, and document refusal objectively.Maintains confidentiality, reduces confrontation, preserves record and future follow-up.

Suggested time

4 hours

Module 5 — Maternal & Infant Priority Cases (CS / ON / PHIV)

Manual reference

CI Field Guide: p.84–91 (CS algorithm + report form). STI Manual: p.122 (ON).
1) Why CS investigations are urgent
  • CS is often preventable with timely maternal screening and treatment.
  • Delays increase risk of missed treatment and reinfection.
  • CI supports closure of gaps: partner services, documentation, follow-up evidence.
2) CS investigation checklist (what you must confirm)

CI Field Guide p.84–91

AreaWhat to verifyWhat to document
Mother ANC pathwayANC attendance, screening dates/resultsDates/results and where done
Maternal treatmentWas treatment given? completed? timing?Regimen dates and completion evidence
Partner servicesWas partner notified and treated?Contact outcomes and dates
Infant pathwayInfant assessment, referral, follow-up planVisits/referrals and next appointment plan
3) Common CS mistakes (and how to avoid them)
  • Missing dates for maternal screening or treatment → capture exact or anchored dates.
  • No partner documentation → reinfection risk remains high without partner action.
  • Infant follow-up not recorded → ensure referral and next step is written clearly.

Suggested time

1.5 hours

Priority lens

CS cases should not sit “pending” without documented action attempts.

1) ON: what it is (CI viewpoint)

STI Manual p.122

CI focus: ON is urgent in newborns. Your job is rapid linkage to clinical care + documentation of action taken.

2) Immediate workflow (what to do)
  1. Confirm caregiver can speak privately and safely.
  2. Link caregiver to clinic/hospital promptly per SOP.
  3. Document date/time, referral destination, and who accepted referral.
  4. Follow up: did they attend? outcome documented?
3) Documentation tips for ON cases
  • Write objective notes: “Caregiver reports eye discharge since yesterday.”
  • Document referral destination + time and next step plan.
  • Keep confidentiality—avoid unnecessary identifying details.

Suggested time

45 minutes

1) PHIV: what CI needs to understand
  • PHIV relates to maternal–infant HIV exposure pathway and follow-up.
  • CI supports: confidential linkage, retention support, partner services, and documentation.
  • Mother–baby cases are high confidentiality and high urgency.
2) PHIV workflow (CI actions)
StepCI actionDocumentation
Referral receivedConfirm identifiers; plan contact safelyReferral date/time + source
LinkageSupport clinic attendance per pathwayAttendance + next appointment plan

3) PHIV communication (safe and confidential)

Use: “I’m calling about an important health follow-up. Can we speak privately?”

Avoid: leaving diagnosis details in voicemail or with relatives.

Module 6 — DHIS2 & Reporting (Comprehensive)

Why this module matters

Your CI work becomes national numbers through reporting. DHIS2 quality depends on accurate source documents and consistent definitions.

1) DHIS2 in one sentence

DHIS2 compiles routine health data—so performance is measured using numbers supported by your source documents.

2) CI data pipeline (field → report)
  1. Source documents: IR/FR, clinic registers, appointment books, lab logs, notification forms.
  2. Tally/summary: monthly CI report sheet or aggregation form.
  3. Data entry: entered into DHIS2 (or sent to the officer who enters).
  4. Validation: checks for missing values, duplicates, outliers.
  5. Use: dashboards, QPRs, supervision, targets, funding decisions.
3) Common CI indicators (what’s usually measured)
Indicator ideaMeaningFrequent confusion
Index interviewedIndex clients successfully interviewedCounting referrals instead of completed interviews
Contacts elicitedTotal contacts named/recordedDouble-counting the same contact without a dedupe method
Contacts notifiedContacts reached/notified per protocolCounting “attempted call” as “notified”
Contacts testedContacts who actually testedCounting “told to test” as tested
Contacts treatedContacts who received treatmentMixing presumptive vs confirmed without definition
4) Data quality checks to do monthly
  • Completeness: all expected fields/totals present.
  • Logic: notified ≤ elicited; tested often ≤ notified (depends on definition).
  • Consistency: outcomes match source records.
  • Duplicates: same contact counted twice?
  • Timeliness: outcomes recorded in correct month.
5) How field mistakes show up in DHIS2
MistakeDHIS2 symptomFix
Weak locator infoLow verified notified/tested/treated outputsImprove locator collection and re-contact where appropriate
Counting attempts as outcomesInflated numbers, later “audit failure”Count only verified outcomes per definition
Missing closuresBacklog and unstable month-to-month totalsClose dispositions per protocol; document clearly

Suggested time

2.5 hours

Key point

DHIS2 “good numbers” require good source documents + verified outcomes.

Module 7 — DIA & CI Flow (Workflow + Practice Scripts)

The DIA cycle (simple and usable)

Use this practical cycle to avoid missed steps: Detect/Define → Investigate → Act (notify, link, document, follow-up).

1) DIA explained (CI in real life)
StepWhat it looks likeWhat you record
D — Detect / Define Receive referral; confirm identifiers; clarify condition; confirm urgency (pregnancy/infant/high risk). Referral source/date, case ID, condition, initial plan.
I — Investigate Interview index; build timeline; elicit contacts; collect locator details; assess barriers/safety. Interview date, critical period assumptions, contact list + locator quality.
A — Act Notify contacts; link to testing/treatment; document outcomes; follow-up; close disposition. Attempts/outcomes, verified testing/treatment, final disposition + closure notes.

Common failure point: completing the interview (I) but not completing Action + closure (A).

2) End-to-end CI flow (no-gaps checklist)
  1. Receive referral → confirm details and urgency.
  2. Plan contact → privacy + safest method/time.
  3. Index interview → confidentiality, timeline, contacts + locator details.
  4. Prioritise → urgent/high risk/hard-to-find first.
  5. Notify contacts → neutral wording, identity protected.
  6. Link to care → testing/treatment attendance support.
  7. Verify outcomes → tested? treated? referred? refused?
  8. Close disposition → clear final status + next steps if needed.
  9. Report → ensure totals match source documents.
3) Interaction practice script — Index interview (read-through)

Goal: Build trust, get a usable timeline, and collect locator details that actually allow follow-up.

CI (you)ClientPurpose
“Hi, my name is ___ from the health team. Is now a private time to talk?” “Yes.” / “Not really.” Confidentiality gate.
“What you share is private. I won’t name anyone else. I’m here to help you protect yourself and others.” “Okay.” Trust + cooperation.
“Let’s focus on the time we need to check. Around when did symptoms start or when did you get tested?” Gives date / “not sure.” Build timeline.
“If you’re not sure, we can use an event. Was it before or after Christmas / school reopening / your birthday?” “After Christmas.” Anchor technique.
“During that time, who did you have sex with? Even if you don’t have the full name, any detail helps.” Names contacts. Elicitation without judgement.
“For each person, what’s the best number, where can they be found, and what time is best?” Gives locator details. Locator quality.
“I will contact them without using your name. Is there anyone who may react violently or put you at risk?” Yes/No. Safety screen.
“What’s the safest way to reach you later? I won’t leave details in messages.” Confirms preference. Follow-up plan.
4) Interaction practice script — Contact notification (read-through)

Rule: Never reveal the index identity or diagnosis. Keep it neutral and service-focused.

CI (you)ContactWhy it works
“Hi, I’m ___ calling from the health team. Is now a private time to speak?” Yes/No. Privacy first.
“You may have been exposed to an infection. We recommend a check-up and testing.” “Exposed how?” Neutral statement.
“I can’t share personal information about anyone else. I can help you access services quickly.” “Alright.” Protects identity + redirects.
“The clinic is open __. Would tomorrow work? If not, what day is best?” Schedules. Moves to action.
“If you have symptoms or concerns, please come sooner. I’ll follow up to confirm you got help.” Agrees. Supports urgency + follow-up.

Suggested time

2 hours

Quick self-check

After every case ask: “Did I VERIFY outcomes or only attempt contact?” Verified outcomes drive real results.
