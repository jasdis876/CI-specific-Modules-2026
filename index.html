Contact Investigation Basics

What is Contact Investigation (CI)?

Contact Investigation (CI) is a public health method used to reduce the spread of infections by identifying people who may have been exposed, helping them get tested, and linking them to treatment.

Core strategy

The goal is to find contacts safely and confidentially, support testing, and prevent re-infection. Each new infected contact may become a new starting point for CI work.

Why CI matters
CI reduces onward transmission, supports early treatment, and strengthens national reporting and surveillance.
Ways contacts are notified
  1. Client referral: the client tells partners and encourages them to attend.
  2. Assisted partner notification: with consent, the provider supports notification, including anonymous options.

Core responsibilities

Contact tracing: identify and notify contacts
Linkage to care: support clinic attendance & treatment
Risk reduction: safer sex counselling & prevention
Records: accurate IR/FR documentation

Program goal

Standardized CI improves outcomes and strengthens surveillance and program reporting across Jamaica.

Confidentiality in CI Work

Why confidentiality is critical

Confidentiality builds trust. If people feel unsafe, they may refuse to name contacts or return for care.

Risks if confidentiality is broken

  • Stigma and discrimination
  • Relationship conflict or violence
  • Loss of trust in health services
  • Reduced program participation

Practical ways to protect privacy

  • Interview in a private setting
  • Use codes (avoid disclosing diagnosis to others)
  • Secure storage of records (paper & digital)
  • Share information only on a need-to-know basis
  • Use respectful language and safe approaches

When safety is a concern

IPV / violence risk

If there is risk of harm, choose safer notification options and involve the clinical team. Document clearly and follow protocol.

Key acronyms

CI: Contact Investigation
IR: Interview Record
FR: Field Record
IC: Index Case

Roles & Responsibilities

What a Contact Investigator does

Key activities

  • Interview clients diagnosed with priority infections
  • Elicit contacts and plan safe notification
  • Initiate FRs and follow up outcomes
  • Support reporting (monthly summaries and indicators)
  • Strengthen linkage to care and re-interview where needed

Timeliness matters

Priority cases should be handled quickly. Track dates and follow-up status carefully to prevent missed opportunities.

Good CI habits

Be accurate: write clear notes
Be safe: avoid outing the client
Be consistent: follow the same process each time

Quality checks

Use validation, double-check codes, and keep timelines visible.

Key Terms

Terms you must know

  • Index Case (IC): the first identified case in an investigation
  • Contact: a person exposed (sexual contact, household contact, etc., depending on condition)
  • Critical Period (CP): time window used to decide which contacts to prioritize
  • Cluster suspect: non-sexual person linked to risk or exposure

Routing & Submission

Forms should be completed neatly, checked for missing fields, and routed according to your parish/region procedures. This platform supports training practice with validation and clean print output.

Before you submit
  • Confirm required fields are complete
  • Confirm codes are correct and protect confidentiality
  • Confirm dates and outcomes are consistent
  • Attach notes if needed (short and factual)
Common mistakes
  • Missing parish code or dates
  • Unreadable notes
  • Not documenting attempts
  • Using names in unsafe ways during notification

Confidentiality reminder

Only share necessary information, using approved approaches and documentation rules.

Form Completion Tips

The “3C” rule

Clear, Complete, Consistent.

Write notes that help you later
Use short sentences: what you did, when you did it, what happened next.
Use dates everywhere
Dates keep investigations defensible and help supervisors review progress.

STI Syndromes & Management (Training)

This section supports CI work: syndromes, coding, and prioritization. Clinical treatment decisions are guided by MOHW clinical algorithms and clinician direction.

Why coding matters

Codes protect confidentiality and help reporting and analysis.

Common syndromes you may see
  • Urethral discharge
  • Vaginal discharge
  • Genital ulcer disease (GUD)
  • Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID)
CI actions after diagnosis
  1. Interview the client (IR)
  2. Determine critical period (CP)
  3. Elicit and document contacts
  4. Initiate FRs and follow outcomes
  5. Support risk reduction counselling

Critical Period tool

Use the CP calculator under CI Basics → Key Terms.

Safety note

Do not disclose diagnosis to contacts. Use safe notification scripts and approved processes.
