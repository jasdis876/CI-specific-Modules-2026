Module 1 — CI Basics (Expanded)

Manual reference

Use the CI Field Guide for deeper reading while you learn here.

CI Field Guide references: p.8–10 (confidentiality + intro).
1) What CI is really trying to achieve
  • Break the chain: exposed people get testing/treatment fast.
  • Reduce reinfection: partners get treated so index doesn’t get it back.
  • Protect privacy: strong confidentiality = better disclosure.
  • Build evidence: your records show what was done and what happened.
2) Confidentiality: the rules + how it sounds in real life

CI Field Guide p.8

Neutral wording examples

  • “I’m calling because you may have been exposed to an infection. We recommend you get checked.”
  • “I can’t share personal information about anyone else, but I can help you access services.”
  • “We keep this private. Your results and details won’t be discussed publicly.”

What to avoid

  • “Your girlfriend/boyfriend gave you…”
  • “X said you…”
  • “Everybody knows you…”
3) Interview quality: what separates average vs strong CI
Average interviewStrong interview
Rushes and collects few partnersUses timeline prompts and “anchor events” to improve recall
Weak locator detailsGets landmarks, best time, work/school, alternate numbers
Vague notesObjective notes + clear next steps + outcomes plan
Client feels judgedSupportive tone → better cooperation
4) Prioritisation: who you act on first (practical)

Rule of thumb: prioritise cases with urgent risk (pregnancy/infant), high spread potential (many partners), and low follow-up probability.

  • Pregnancy + syphilis → urgent (prevents congenital syphilis).
  • Newborn concerns (eye discharge, suspected congenital infection) → urgent referral and documentation.
  • Hard-to-find contacts: act quickly before phones change/people move.

Suggested time

2.5 hours

Golden rule

If your words could expose identity/diagnosis, rephrase before you speak or write.

Module 4 — STI Management (Expanded)

Manual reference

STI Manual references: p.131–133 (treatment checklist), plus syndrome sections.
1) CI role around treatment (what you do / don’t do)

Scope clarity: Clinicians prescribe. CI supports partner services, linkage, prevention education, and documentation.

  • Confirm partner notification plan and assist tracing.
  • Support contacts to attend clinic for testing/treatment.
  • Prevention messages to reduce reinfection.
  • Document outcomes clearly.
2) STI conditions covered in services (broad list)
  • Syphilis (all stages) + congenital
  • Gonorrhoea, chlamydia
  • Trichomoniasis / BV, candidiasis
  • Genital herpes (HSV), genital warts (HPV)
  • Chancroid, LGV, granuloma inguinale
  • PID, epididymo-orchitis
  • ON (newborn eye infection)
3) Treatment summary (always verify in STI Manual)

STI Manual p.131–133

Important: This section is a high-level summary so CI understands the workflow. Exact regimens/alternatives/pregnancy adjustments must be verified in the STI Manual.

ConditionCommon treatment idea (summary)CI follow-up priority
GonorrhoeaCommonly ceftriaxone IM stat (see manual)Trace partners quickly; confirm clinic attendance
ChlamydiaDoxycycline course or alternative (see manual)Partner management + reinfection prevention
Trichomoniasis/BVMetronidazole regimens (see manual)Adherence + return if persistent symptoms
SyphilisBPG regimen depends on stage (see manual)Correct stage timeframe + partner services
PIDCombination therapy (see manual)Urgent follow-up; ensure partner services
HSV / HPVAntivirals/management (see manual)Counselling + prevention education
4) Reinfection prevention: the key message set
  • “If your partner doesn’t get treated, you can get it back.”
  • “Follow the clinic advice about when to restart sex.”
  • “Use condoms every time to lower risk.”
  • “Come back if symptoms return.”

Module 5 — Maternal & Infant Priority Cases (CS / ON / PHIV)

Manual reference

CI Field Guide: p.84–91 (CS algorithm + report form). STI Manual: p.122 (ON).
1) Why CS investigations are urgent
  • CS is often preventable with timely maternal screening and treatment.
  • When missed, infant outcomes can be severe—so delays are high risk.
  • CI helps close gaps: partner services, documentation, follow-up evidence.
2) CS investigation checklist (what you must confirm)

CI Field Guide p.84–91

AreaWhat to verifyWhat to document
Mother ANC pathwayANC attendance, screening dates/resultsDates/results and where done
Maternal treatmentWas treatment given? completed? timing?Regimen dates and completion evidence
Partner servicesWas partner notified and treated?Contact outcomes and dates
Infant pathwayInfant assessment, referral, follow-up planVisits/referrals and next appointment plan
3) Common CS mistakes (and how to avoid them)
  • Missing dates for maternal screening or treatment → always capture exact or anchored dates.
  • No partner documentation → partner services strongly affect reinfection risk.
  • Infant follow-up not recorded → ensure referral and next step is written clearly.

Suggested time

1.5 hours

Priority lens

CS cases should not sit “pending” without documented action attempts.

1) ON: what it is (CI viewpoint)

STI Manual p.122

CI focus: ON is urgent in newborns. Your job is rapid linkage to clinical care + documentation of action taken.

2) What to do immediately (workflow)
  1. Confirm infant symptoms and identify safest way to get baby seen promptly.
  2. Link caregiver to clinic/hospital as advised in SOP.
  3. Document date/time, referral destination, and who accepted referral.
  4. Record follow-up: did they attend? what was outcome documented?
3) Documentation tips for ON cases
  • Write objective notes: “Caregiver reports eye discharge since yesterday.”
  • Document “referred to ____ at ____ time” and “follow-up plan.”
  • Keep confidentiality—avoid unnecessary personal details.

Suggested time

45 minutes

1) PHIV: what CI needs to understand
  • PHIV refers to HIV linked to pregnancy/birth and infant exposure pathway.
  • CI work supports: rapid linkage, follow-up, partner services, and documentation.
  • Mother–baby cases are high confidentiality and high urgency.
2) PHIV workflow (CI actions)
StepCI actionDocumentation
Referral receivedConfirm key identifiers; plan contact safelyReferral date/time + source
LinkageSupport clinic attendance for mother/baby per programme pathwayAttendance outcome + next appointment
Partner servicesSupport notification and testing of partner(s) per policyContacts + outcomes
Retention supportRemind follow-up visits; reduce missed appointmentsAttempts + client response
3) PHIV communication (safe, respectful, confidential)

Use: “I’m calling about an important health follow-up. Can we speak privately?”

Avoid: leaving diagnosis details in voicemail or with relatives.

Suggested time

45 minutes

Module 6 — DHIS2 & Reporting (Comprehensive)

Why this module matters

Your CI work becomes national numbers through reporting. DHIS2 quality depends on accurate source documents and consistent definitions.

1) DHIS2 in one sentence

DHIS2 is the system used to compile and analyse routine health data—so programme performance is measured using the numbers you help generate.

2) CI data pipeline (from field work to report)
  1. Source documents: IR/FR, clinic registers, appointment books, lab logs, notification forms.
  2. Tally/summary: monthly CI report sheet or aggregation form.
  3. Data entry: entered into DHIS2 (or sent to the officer who enters).
  4. Validation: checks for missing values, duplicates, outliers.
  5. Use: dashboards, QPRs, supervision, targets, funding decisions.
3) Common CI indicators (what’s usually measured)
Indicator ideaWhat it meansCommon error
Index interviewedNumber of index clients successfully interviewedCounting referrals instead of completed interviews
Contacts elicitedTotal contacts named/recordedDouble-counting same person across cases without dedupe method
Contacts notifiedContacts reached and notified per protocolCounting “attempted call” as notified
Contacts testedContacts who actually testedCounting “told to test” as tested
Contacts treatedContacts who received treatmentMixing presumptive vs confirmed without definition
Disposition completenessCases with final outcomes recordedLeaving cases open forever (“pending”)
4) Data quality (DQ) checks you should do every month

Quick DQ checklist

  • Completeness: are all expected sections filled?
  • Consistency: contacts notified ≤ contacts elicited (usually).
  • Logic: contacts tested ≤ contacts notified (usually).
  • Duplicates: same contact counted twice?
  • Dates: are interviews/attempts in the correct month?
5) How CI mistakes show up in DHIS2 (real-world)
MistakeWhat DHIS2 showsFix
Weak locator infoLow “notified/tested/treated” outputsImprove locator detail collection; re-interview where allowed
Counting attempts as outcomesInflated performance then “audit failure” laterOnly count verified outcomes
Missing closuresBacklog; inconsistent monthly totalsFollow closure protocol; update dispositions
Late entryMonth-to-month spikes/dropsStick to reporting rhythm; submit by deadline
6) Reporting rhythm (how to stay on top of your month)
  1. Weekly: update FR outcomes; close what can be closed.
  2. End of month: reconcile with registers and lab sources.
  3. Before submission: run the DQ checklist and fix issues.
  4. After submission: keep a copy; note key challenges for QPR.

Suggested time

2.5 hours

Key point

DHIS2 only looks “good” when source documents are complete and outcomes are verified.
