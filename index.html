Module 1 — CI Basics

Manual reference

Use the manuals as your guide while you learn. Download them in Resources.

CI Field Guide references for this module: PDF p.8–10 and PDF p.54 (programme expectations).

Learning outcomes

  • Explain what CI is and why it matters.
  • Apply confidentiality rules in real situations.
  • Describe the CI workflow from referral to closure.
  • Recognize safety and professionalism standards.
1) What CI is (core purpose)

CI Field Guide PDF p.9

Contact Investigation (CI) is a structured public health process used to stop infections from spreading.

  • Interview the person diagnosed (index case).
  • Identify contacts who may have been exposed.
  • Notify contacts without revealing identity/diagnosis.
  • Support contacts to test and treat (linkage/referral).
  • Document actions and outcomes (IR + FR) and report indicators.
2) Confidentiality (non-negotiable rules)

CI Field Guide PDF p.8

Non-negotiable

Never reveal the index client’s diagnosis or identity to a partner/contact.

  • Use private settings for interviews and notifications.
  • Secure paper files (no open desks, no vehicle seat storage).
  • Use approved codes and objective wording.
  • Share information on a strict need-to-know basis.
3) Basic CI workflow (what happens step-by-step)

CI Field Guide PDF p.9–11

  1. Receive referral (clinic/lab/programme).
  2. Prepare (review info, plan safe approach, materials).
  3. Interview (rapport → explain purpose → collect contacts).
  4. Open FRs for contacts; plan attempts.
  5. Link contacts to services (testing, treatment, counselling).
  6. Close & report with complete documentation.
4) Targets & expectations (what CIs are expected to do)

CI Field Guide PDF p.53–54

  • Locate/interview persons diagnosed (priority conditions get priority).
  • Support partner notification and linkage to services.
  • Ensure records are accurate (data clerks can only enter what you document).
  • Support reporting and programme monitoring.

Suggested time

2 hours

Read, expand each section, take notes, and practice scenario wording.

Quick memory anchors

  • Protect identity
  • Document everything
  • Notify safely
  • Link to testing/treatment

1) Interview Record (IR) — what it captures

CI Field Guide PDF p.12–17

  • Index client details (as per programme rules).
  • Interview details (date, place, interviewer/CI code).
  • Risk window & contacts named (with locator info).
  • HIV test history/referral details if relevant.
  • Objective notes to support follow-up.
2) Field Record (FR) — what it tracks

CI Field Guide PDF p.10–11

  • Contact locator details (address, phone, landmarks).
  • All attempts (date/time/method/outcome).
  • Final disposition (tested/treated/refused/not found/moved).

Module 3 — Critical Period Interview Tool

Manual reference

Critical period guidance + maximum infectious period table are in the CI Field Guide.

CI Field Guide: PDF p.41 (critical period definition + table)
1) Teaching: What is the “critical period”?

CI Field Guide PDF p.41

The critical period is the time window when a person could have been infectious and exposed partners/contacts. In interviews, it helps you decide which partners to focus on.

Rule used in this tool: Critical Period = (Maximum infectious period) + (duration of signs/symptoms before treatment).

Tip: If symptom duration is unknown, document that and follow programme guidance for best estimate.

Critical Period Calculator
