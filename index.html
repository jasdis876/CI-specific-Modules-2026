Module 1 — CI Basics (Ultimate Refresher)

Manual reference

This module includes expanded explanations. Use the CI Field Guide for deeper detail.

CI Field Guide references: PDF p.8–10 (confidentiality + intro), plus programme expectations PDF p.53–54.

What a strong CI does (in simple terms)

  • Find the right people (index + contacts).
  • Protect privacy (never expose identity/diagnosis).
  • Support action (testing, treatment, counselling, prevention).
  • Write it down properly (records are evidence of work and outcomes).
1) Definitions you must know

These words show up in reports, interviews, and supervisor reviews. Knowing them makes you faster and more accurate.

TermMeaning (CI use)
Index case/clientThe diagnosed person you interview to identify contacts.
ContactPerson who may have been exposed and needs notification, testing and/or treatment.
Partner notificationLetting a contact know they may have been exposed, without identifying the index client.
Disposition/outcomeThe final result of the contact investigation (tested, treated, refused, not found, moved, etc.).
Locator informationDetails that help you find someone: phone, address, landmarks, workplace, best time, etc.
2) Confidentiality rules (what to do and what to never do)

CI Field Guide PDF p.8

Never do these

  • Never tell a contact who named them.
  • Never reveal the diagnosis/condition of the index client.
  • Never discuss cases in public spaces (waiting rooms, taxis, shops).
  • Never leave forms open in vehicles or on desks.

Do these instead

  • Use private interview locations.
  • Use neutral wording: “You may have been exposed” (not “You got X from…”).
  • Use programme codes and factual notes.
  • Share information only with authorised staff who need it for care/public health.
3) The CI workflow (from referral to closure)

CI Field Guide PDF p.9–11

  1. Receive referral and confirm the condition/priority.
  2. Prepare: review info, plan safest approach, gather materials.
  3. Interview: build trust, explain confidentiality, collect contacts + locator details.
  4. Notify contacts: choose method (phone/field/clinic), do not expose identity/diagnosis.
  5. Link to services: testing, treatment, counselling, prevention education.
  6. Document attempts/outcomes so the story is clear on paper.
  7. Close case: final dispositions recorded and ready for reporting.

Supervisor lens

Strong CIs are judged by: quality of interviews, completeness of locator details, confidentiality, and documented outcomes.

4) Interview structure (simple “script” you can follow)

A practical CI interview flow

  1. Rapport: “I’m here to support you. This is confidential.”
  2. Purpose: “We try to protect you and others by finding who may need testing/treatment.”
  3. Safety/confidentiality: explain that you won’t expose them to partners.
  4. Timeline: identify symptom start/treatment date for partner period.
  5. Partner list: names/aliases, phones, addresses, landmarks, best times.
  6. Plan: ask how they want partners notified (options within policy).
  7. Prevention: condom use, return for retesting, avoid reinfection.
  8. Close: recap next steps and confirm follow-up method.
5) Prioritisation (who you go to first)

Priority principle

Prioritise cases with higher risk of transmission, severe outcomes, or urgent public health impact.

  • Pregnancy/infant-related cases (e.g., congenital syphilis risk) should be urgent.
  • Cases with multiple partners or low likelihood of return to clinic need quick action.
  • Cases with safety risks require planned approach and supervisor support.

Suggested time

2.5 hours

Read each section and practice the interview flow aloud.

Golden rule

If your wording could expose identity/diagnosis, rephrase before you speak or write.

Module 2 — Forms & Records (Field-by-Field Guide)

Manual reference

This module explains what each form section is for and how to avoid common mistakes.

CI Field Guide: IR PDF p.12–16, explanation p.17, FR guidance p.10–11, Forms procedures p.37, Class 1 Notification Form p.110, Congenital Syphilis Report Form p.89.
1) Why forms matter (programme reality)

Forms are evidence

  • If it is not documented, it is treated like it did not happen.
  • Quality documentation supports reporting, funding, supervision, and performance reviews.
  • Good forms protect the client, the CI, and the programme.
2) Interview Record (IR) — what each section is trying to capture

CI Field Guide PDF p.12–17

IR sectionPurposeCommon mistakes
Client identifiersCorrectly link the IR to the right person.Wrong spelling, missing DOB, unclear ID.
Interview detailsShow when/where and who conducted the interview.No date, no CI code, vague location.
Risk/timelineDefine the partner period and key dates (symptoms, testing, treatment).No timeline, mixed-up dates, not documenting “unknown”.
Contacts namedRecord partners/contacts with usable locator details.Only “nickname”, no phone/address/landmark.
Plan & notesClear next steps and factual notes for follow-up.Judgmental language, confidential details that expose identity.
3) Field Record (FR) — attempt tracking and outcomes

CI Field Guide PDF p.10–11

FR best practice

  • Every attempt gets a date/time/method and result.
  • Record the “best time to find” and “best contact method.”
  • Final disposition must be clear (tested/treated/refused/not found/moved).
4) Other investigation forms (teach points)

5) Documentation quality rules (what supervisors check)

Quality checklist

  • All key dates present (interview, attempts, outcomes).
  • Locator details are usable (not vague).
  • Notes are factual and confidential.
  • Outcomes are clearly stated (no “pending” forever).
  • Corrections are handled properly (no messy rewriting that destroys meaning).

Suggested time

2.5 hours

Read the field-by-field guide and compare the official forms in the PDF.

Biggest issue in CI reports

Missing dates + weak locator details = investigation slows down and outcomes drop.

Module 3 — Critical Period Interview (Teaching + Tool)

Manual reference

Critical period guidance + maximum infectious period table are in the CI Field Guide.

CI Field Guide: PDF p.41
1) What is “critical period” and why it matters

CI Field Guide PDF p.41

  • It helps you decide which partners to focus on for notification and follow-up.
  • It makes the interview clearer: “We are focusing on contacts from [start date] to now.”
  • It supports fairness and consistency across cases.

Simple explanation you can tell a client

“We are going to focus on the time period when you could have passed this infection to someone else. That helps us protect you and protect them.”

2) When dates are unknown (document it properly)

Real life issue

Some clients do not know exact dates. That is common. The key is documenting “unknown” clearly and using best estimate per guidance.

  • If symptom start is unknown: document “unknown” and use best estimate method.
  • If partner dates are unknown: anchor using events (“around Christmas”, “after school reopened”).
  • Always record your assumptions in notes.

Module 4 — STI Management (CI Actions + Treatments Summary)

Manual reference

This module summarises the most used STI treatments and what the CI should do around them.

STI Manual references: PDF p.131–133 (treatment checklist), p.49–51 (abnormal vaginal discharge), p.82 (GUD/syphilis), p.122 (ON).
1) CI role in STI management (what you do vs what clinician does)

Scope clarity

Clinicians prescribe treatment. CI supports investigation, partner notification, linkage to services, and documentation.

  • Confirm partner notification plan and assist with contact tracing.
  • Support contacts to attend clinic for testing/treatment.
  • Provide prevention education to reduce reinfection (condoms, retesting, partner treatment).
  • Document outcomes clearly (tested/treated/refused/not found).
  • Escalate urgent situations (pregnancy, infant eye infection, severe symptoms) per protocol.
2) STI conditions commonly handled in STI services
  • Syphilis (primary, secondary, early latent, late latent, tertiary) + congenital syphilis
  • Gonorrhoea, chlamydia, trichomoniasis/BV, candidiasis
  • Genital herpes (HSV), genital warts (HPV)
  • Chancroid, LGV, granuloma inguinale
  • Ophthalmia neonatorum (newborn eye infection)
3) Treatment checklist summary (quick reference)

STI Manual PDF p.131–133

Note: This is a summary. Verify details, pregnancy considerations, and alternatives in the STI Manual pages above.

4) Communication that prevents reinfection (client-friendly)

Simple prevention message

  • “If your partner does not get treated, you can get it back again.”
  • “Avoid sex until you and your partner finish treatment (clinic advice).”
  • “Use condoms every time to reduce risk.”
  • “Return for follow-up if symptoms come back.”

Suggested time

4 hours

Read this summary, then cross-check the STI Manual sections cited.

High priority situations

Pregnancy, infants, severe symptoms, and poor follow-up risk should be escalated per protocol.

Module 5 — Congenital Syphilis & Ophthalmia Neonatorum (Urgent Pathway)

Manual reference

CI Field Guide: PDF p.84–91 (algorithm + guidance + report form). STI Manual: PDF p.122 (ON).
1) Why congenital syphilis is treated as urgent
  • It is preventable when maternal testing and treatment happens on time.
  • It can lead to serious harm for the baby if missed.
  • CI helps close gaps: partner management, maternal treatment evidence, infant follow-up documentation.
2) Investigation focus (what you must confirm)

CI Field Guide PDF p.88–91

  • Maternal ANC attendance and syphilis screening history.
  • Maternal treatment: was it given, when, and was it completed?
  • Partner management: was partner notified/treated?
  • Infant assessment and follow-up plan.
  • Documentation completeness using the Congenital Syphilis Investigation Report Form.
3) Ophthalmia Neonatorum (ON): what CI should know

STI Manual PDF p.122

CI teaching point

A newborn with eye discharge is urgent. The CI role is rapid linkage and documentation—ensure the baby is seen promptly by clinical care.

4) Documentation and confidentiality (mother–baby cases)

Sensitive case warning

Keep records secure. Keep wording factual. Share information only with authorized care/public health staff.

  • Avoid blame wording. Document actions and dates.
  • Ensure mother and partner pathway is clearly recorded.
  • Record referral completion where possible (attended clinic, treated, etc.).

Suggested time

1.5 hours

Review the algorithm in the Field Guide and write your “urgent pathway” actions.
