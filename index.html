CI Basics

What is Contact Investigation?

The CI Field Guide describes CI as a standardized set of field operations to support control of STIs and other infectious diseases, focusing on contact tracing, partner notification, testing, and provider referral.

Why CI matters

Control of STIs can’t be achieved only by treating people who show up with symptoms; CI helps break transmission by finding, testing and treating contacts, while maintaining confidentiality.

Partner Notification: 2 main routes

Client-led referral vs Assisted Provider Notification

  • Client-led: counselling + interviewing motivates the patient to bring contacts for evaluation/treatment.
  • Assisted provider: with consent, a trained provider supports disclosure or anonymous notification and offers testing. Approaches include contract referral, provider referral, or dual referral.

Confidentiality & Safety

Non-negotiable

Confidentiality is “paramount” because breaches can cause stigma, discrimination, and even intimate partner violence. Practical strategies include private interviewing, coding diagnosis, and secure storage/access protocols.

Professionalism is the best protection

The guide emphasizes that the character, judgment, integrity, and sensitivity of the CI is the strongest guarantee of privacy.

Key Acronyms (from the guide)

  • CI: Contact Investigator
  • IR: Interview Record
  • FR: Field Record
  • IC / CC / CF: Index Case / Case Closure / Case File copies
  • CP: Critical Period
  • DIA: Direct Interview Approach
  • MSSR: Monthly Summary Statistics Report
  • PrEP: Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis

Quick operational reminders

  • Use coding to protect diagnoses.
  • Do interviews in private.
  • Only share data per MOHW protocols and legal framework.

Forms & Records: Interview Record (IR)

Purpose of the Interview Record (IR)

  • Compiles information about contacts/suspected contacts for analysis and planning.
  • Informs parish surveillance about patients with priority infections.
  • Helps prepare the Monthly Summary Statistics Report (MSSR).
  • Permanent record of interviews and outcomes for future investigations.

IR Routing & Case Closure (Important)

IR is completed in triplicate: IC (submitted after interview), CC (submitted at closure), CF (CI keeps as permanent record).

Cases are generally closed when all FRs are dispositioned. The guide notes a cut-off after at least four reasonable attempts to investigate—typically minimum three calls and at least three visits, usually within a three-month period.

Field-by-field reminders (from the guide)

  • CI #/Name: use the 4-character CI code (e.g., SE02) + your name.
  • Parish: 3-letter code for where the patient lives.
  • STI codes: write all infections on one IR.
  • # sex contacts named: not the same as # initiated on FR (important distinction).
  • Critical period contacts: include date of last sex (LSI), sex, FR initiated Y/N, and parish to be worked.
  • Disposition: close when contacts/suspects dispositioned with supervisor concurrence; attach closed FRs to CC IR copy.

Closure attempts

The guide indicates a typical closure cut-off after at least 3 calls and 3 visits (within about 3 months) if investigation cannot be completed.

Download tip

JSON is best for saving/rehydrating the form later. CSV is best for Excel. For a “PDF”, use Print → Save as PDF.

Clinical & Syndromes (CI-relevant)

Priority conditions for investigation

Priority A conditions should receive highest investigation priority:

  • HIV/AIDS (C13)
  • Pregnant women with reactive serology
  • Early congenital syphilis (< 1 year) (A7)
  • Infectious syphilis (A1, A2, A3)
  • Ophthalmia neonatorum
  • STI in childhood

Priority B examples:

  • Severe PID
  • Contacts / Referrals
  • Hepatitis B
  • Resistant gonorrhea
  • Other Genital Ulcer Disease (GUD)

STI coding (quick reference)

Coding supports confidentiality in records and CI.

  • A1 Primary syphilis, A2 Secondary, A3 Early latent, A6 Late/late latent, A7 Congenital < 1 year
  • B1 Post-pubertal gonorrhea
  • C10 Genital herpes
  • C13 HIV/AIDS

CI timeline standards (from targets & expectations)

  • Priority cases: assign to a CI and complete a field visit within 72 hours.
  • Class 1 notifiable illnesses: report within 24 hours of suspicion/confirmation.
  • Outputs: investigative outputs should be completed/submitted within 6 weeks (targets include interviewing new cases and locating/testing locatable contacts).

This section is intentionally “CI-relevant” (what you need to do/record/route). For the full clinical algorithms, open the Field Guide PDF and use the syndromes/algorithms pages.

Reporting flow reminder

Monthly CI/STI reports are entered into DHIS2. Regional reporting is due on the 5th working day of the following month. If late, an explanation should be provided.
